Relieve pain and protect your oral health with careful evaluation and removal of problematic wisdom teeth.

When Wisdom Teeth May Need Removal

Wisdom teeth do not always need to be removed, but extraction may be recommended when they are causing—or are likely to cause—problems such as:

  • Pain or tenderness in the back of the mouth
  • Swollen, irritated, or infected gum tissue
  • Wisdom teeth trapped beneath the gums or bone
  • Crowding or pressure against nearby teeth
  • Cavities in a wisdom tooth or the tooth next to it
  • Difficulty cleaning the area effectively
  • Cysts or damage to surrounding structures

What Is an Impacted Wisdom Tooth?

A wisdom tooth is impacted when it does not have enough room to emerge normally. It may remain partly or completely beneath the gums, grow at an angle, or press against another tooth.

Does Every Wisdom Tooth Need Removal?

No. Wisdom teeth that are healthy, fully erupted, easy to clean, and not damaging nearby structures may only need continued monitoring during routine exams.

Evaluation Comes Before Extraction

Wisdom tooth treatment begins with an exam and appropriate imaging. Dr. Dawson will review the position of the teeth, nearby nerves, bone, gums, and neighboring teeth before explaining whether monitoring, extraction in our office, or referral to an oral surgeon is appropriate.

What to Expect During Treatment

  1. 1

    We review your health history, medications, symptoms, and any concerns about treatment.

  2. 2

    Dr. Dawson examines the area and reviews the position of your wisdom teeth using appropriate digital imaging.

  3. 3

    We explain the procedure, expected recovery, possible risks, and available comfort options.

  4. 4

    The area is thoroughly numbed before treatment begins.

  5. 5

    The wisdom tooth is carefully loosened and removed. A tooth beneath the gums may require a small opening and sectioning into smaller pieces.

  6. 6

    The area is cleaned, and stitches may be placed when needed.

  7. 7

    You receive written instructions for protecting the area and supporting healing at home.

Nervous About Wisdom Tooth Removal?

Tell us about any anxiety when scheduling your visit. We use a comfort-focused approach and can discuss nitrous oxide sedation when appropriate. More complex cases may be referred to an oral surgeon who offers additional sedation options.

Recovery After Wisdom Tooth Extraction

Some soreness, swelling, and limited jaw movement can be expected after wisdom tooth removal. Follow the instructions provided by your dental team, rest, use cold compresses as directed, eat soft foods, and avoid smoking, straws, vigorous rinsing, or disturbing the extraction site during early healing. Contact the office if symptoms become severe or do not improve as expected.

Common questions.

How do I know if my wisdom teeth need to be removed?

An exam and X-rays are needed to evaluate their position and health. Pain, infection, decay, gum problems, crowding, or damage to nearby teeth may indicate that removal should be considered.

Does wisdom tooth extraction hurt?

The area is numbed before treatment, so you should not feel sharp pain during the procedure. Pressure and movement are normal. Temporary soreness and swelling may occur afterward.

How long does recovery take?

Many patients begin feeling better within several days, although the extraction sites continue healing for longer. Recovery varies based on the number and position of the teeth and the complexity of treatment.

What can I eat after wisdom tooth removal?

Begin with soft, cool, or lukewarm foods such as yogurt, applesauce, eggs, mashed potatoes, and smoothies eaten without a straw. Gradually return to firmer foods as comfort improves.

What is dry socket?

Dry socket can occur when the blood clot protecting the extraction site is lost or disrupted. It may cause increasing pain several days after removal. Smoking, straws, vigorous rinsing, and disturbing the area can increase the risk.

When should I call after an extraction?

Contact the office for severe or worsening pain, heavy bleeding, increasing swelling, fever, difficulty breathing or swallowing, a bad taste with drainage, or any symptom that concerns you. Breathing or swallowing difficulty requires immediate emergency medical attention.

Can Dawson Dentistry remove all wisdom teeth?

Some wisdom teeth can be removed in our office. Complex impactions, teeth close to important nerves, or cases requiring deeper sedation may be referred to a trusted oral surgeon.
