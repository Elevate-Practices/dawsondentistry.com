How Denture Treatment Works

  1. 1

    We begin with a consultation to discuss your concerns, current teeth, health history, and goals for your smile.

  2. 2

    Dr. Dawson evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, jaw structure, and any existing dentures or dental work.

  3. 3

    We determine whether a full denture, partial denture, implant-supported option, or another treatment is most appropriate.

  4. 4

    Impressions or digital records are taken so your restoration can be designed for your mouth and facial features.

  5. 5

    Your teeth, bite, and appearance are reviewed during the fitting process, and adjustments are made as needed.

  6. 6

    Your final denture or partial is delivered with instructions for wearing, cleaning, and adapting to it.

What Is a Full Denture?

A full denture replaces all teeth in the upper arch, lower arch, or both. It rests on the gums and is customized to support your facial appearance and restore everyday function.

What Is a Partial Denture?

A partial denture replaces several missing teeth while using the remaining natural teeth for support. It helps fill open spaces and may reduce unwanted movement of neighboring teeth.

Designed for More Than Appearance

Dentures and partials can improve the appearance of your smile, but they also help restore chewing, speech, facial support, and confidence. Careful planning and follow-up adjustments are important for achieving a comfortable and functional result.

Which Denture Option Is Right for You?

The best option depends on how many teeth are missing, the condition of your remaining teeth, the health of your gums and jawbone, your budget, and whether you prefer a removable or implant-supported solution. We will explain your options clearly and help you compare their benefits, limitations, treatment timelines, and maintenance needs.

Could Dental Implants Improve Denture Stability?

Some dentures can be supported or retained by dental implants , which may improve stability and confidence while speaking and eating. Implant-supported treatment is not right for every patient, so we will evaluate your health, bone support, goals, and treatment preferences.

Common questions.

What is the difference between dentures and partials?

A full denture replaces all teeth in an upper or lower arch. A partial denture replaces several missing teeth while working around the natural teeth that remain.

How long does it take to get dentures?

Timing varies depending on whether teeth need to be removed, whether healing is required, and the type of denture being made. Treatment often requires several appointments for records, fittings, and adjustments.

Will dentures look natural?

Denture teeth and gums can be customized for shade, shape, size, and arrangement to complement your facial features and create a natural, balanced appearance.

Will it take time to get used to new dentures?

Yes. New dentures may initially affect speech, chewing, and the way your mouth feels. Most patients improve with practice, time, and any needed follow-up adjustments.

Can I eat normally with dentures?

Dentures can improve chewing, but they do not function exactly like natural teeth. Patients often begin with softer foods, smaller bites, and chewing on both sides while they adapt.

How should I clean my dentures or partials?

Removable dentures should be cleaned daily with products and techniques recommended by your dental team. Your gums, tongue, and any remaining natural teeth also need consistent cleaning.

Do dentures need to be adjusted or replaced?

Yes. The mouth and jaw can change over time, which may affect fit. Dentures may eventually require adjustment, relining, repair, or replacement to maintain comfort and function.
